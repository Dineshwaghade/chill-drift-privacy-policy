Chill Drift — Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 25, 2026

Chill Drift ("the app", "the game") is developed by Waghade Studio ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the app interacts with, why, and the choices you have. Chill Drift is an offline endless-runner game — it does not require an account and does not collect your name, email address, or any other directly identifying information.

Information stored on your device

The app saves your game progress locally on your device only. This includes:

  • High scores and best distance
  • Collected in-game currency (snowflakes)
  • Unlocked and selected characters and boards
  • Sound, music, and haptics preferences

Your game progress stays on your device. We do not operate a server that receives, stores, or has access to it, and it is removed if you uninstall the app or clear its data. (Crash reports are a separate matter and are described below — they never include your game progress.)

Advertising

Chill Drift shows interstitial ads between game sessions using Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK). To serve and measure ads, Google's SDK may collect and process information such as:

  • Advertising identifier and other device identifiers
  • IP address and approximate, coarse location derived from it
  • Device and app information (device model, OS version, app version, language)
  • Ad interaction data (impressions, clicks, and similar engagement signals)

This processing is governed by Google's own privacy policy and terms, not by us directly — we do not receive or store this data ourselves. You can learn more and review your options at:

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use AdMob

On Android, you can view or reset your advertising ID, and opt out of personalized (interest-based) ads, from your device's Settings → Google → Ads (or Settings → Privacy → Ads, depending on your Android version).

Crash and error reporting

To find and fix the bugs that make the game crash or misbehave, Chill Drift uses Firebase Crashlytics (a Google service). It sends a report only when the app hits an error — it does not monitor how you play, and it sends nothing during normal play.

A crash report contains:

  • The error itself: its type, message, and the lines of code it came from
  • Device and app information (device model and manufacturer, Android version, app version, CPU type, available memory and storage, screen orientation)
  • Whether the app was in the foreground, and how long it had been running
  • A randomly generated installation identifier, which lets us tell "one person hit this 50 times" apart from "50 people hit this once"

It does not include your name, email address, or any account details — the game has no accounts — and it does not include your game progress, your snowflakes, or the advertising identifier described in the previous section. The installation identifier is specific to Crashlytics, is not your advertising ID, and is reset if you uninstall the app or clear its data.

Google processes this data on our behalf as a service provider. Crash reports are retained for 90 days, after which Google begins removing them from its live and backup systems. We use them only to diagnose and fix problems in the app, and we do not sell them or use them for advertising.

Children's privacy

Chill Drift is a general-audience casual game and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with information, please contact us using the details below so we can address it. Because the app serves ads via Google AdMob, ad personalization for any users identified as children is handled according to Google's own child-directed treatment and applicable regulations (such as COPPA).

Data security

Your gameplay data is stored only on your device and the app does not transmit it to us, so there is no developer-operated server or database holding your game progress to secure.

The crash reports described above are the one thing the app does send. They are transmitted over an encrypted connection and held in our Firebase project, where access is limited to the developer. Data handled by the advertising and crash reporting SDKs is protected under Google's own security practices.
